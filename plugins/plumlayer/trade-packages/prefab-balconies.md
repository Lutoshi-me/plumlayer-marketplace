# Prefabricated balconies

Trade knowledge for an agent about to read a drawing set and produce scope for the prefabricated
balcony and associated railing package. Everything here is learned from real subcontractor proposals
and estimator leveling sheets. The entry speaks in calibrated plain language: how common a behavior
is, is carried by the words themselves. Where real proposals have not shown something, this file says
so.

Read this before reading the drawings. Two things to carry in. This is a supply-only trade in which
the installer is a separate buy and often a separate ranking. And the manufacturers price their own
standard product, say so in writing on the face of the proposal, and leave the difference between
their catalogue and the drawings for someone else to reconcile.

---

## 1. Trade identity and packaging

The package is bid under a division 05 balconies and railings section where the estimator gives it
one, and under a division 05 metal fabrications section where it does not. Both shapes occur, and the
choice is consequential: a dedicated tab normalizes on balcony count, while a metal fabrications tab
normalizes on project gross square footage, so a per-balcony rate lifted from one is not comparable
with a rate lifted from the other. Establish which shape the sheet is before reading any number.

Nearly every bidder is an aluminum balcony and railing manufacturer selling supply only. That is the
standard bid in this market and it is not a partial bid. Two other kinds appear: architectural metal
fabricators who fabricate, deliver and will install their own railing, and cladding or exterior
installers bidding the install half against somebody else's material.

Bid lists in this trade look deeper than they are. On one project a manufacturer disclosed in its
covering email that a competing bidder on the same list was carrying its proposal, and that its own
sister company, bidding the cladding package, might also carry it for installation. So three columns
on that ranking trace back to one material source. Establish the material source behind each column
before treating them as independent offers.

What collapses into the package:

- The balcony railing, always. The deck and its rail are one product and ship as one order, though the
  rail is attached in the field.
- Terrace, patio, courtyard and roof deck railings, usually, because the same extrusion and the same
  shop make them. This is where the package's boundary becomes elastic; see section 5.
- The knife plates, tie rods, clevises, brackets and post bases that attach the balcony to the
  building. Supplied by the manufacturer, installed by others, into blocking provided by a third
  party. The manufacturer supplies them as steel components in an otherwise aluminum package.
- Engineering, PE-stamped submittals and shop drawings.
- Glass-infill railing, where it is the same manufacturer's extrusion with a glass panel. Sometimes
  routed to glazing instead.
- Gates in railing runs, priced as their own item at a rate per foot far above plain rail.
- Privacy screens, fencing and pergola or cabana frames, occasionally, as extensions of the same
  extrusion family.

What leaves the package:

- Installation, in nearly every case.
- Thermal breaks at the balcony connection, which are a detail on the architectural set and appear on
  the leveling sheet as an allowance and a plug rather than as anyone's bid item.
- Ballasted, non-penetrating roof guardrail, which is bid as its own package and is carried by the
  metals trade entry. It appears on this trade's leveling sheet only as a cross-package add row, and
  the boundary between a balcony rail and a roof egress rail is the largest quantity dispute in the
  package.
- Balcony doors, which are bid inside the window package.
- Wood slats in pergola and cabana frames, where the manufacturer offers to fabricate the frame to
  receive them.

## 2. How the market bids

This is one of the most heavily itemized supply trades in the corpus. The proposals are twenty to
thirty pages, most of it product description and terms, with a fully unit-priced schedule at the
front.

The itemization is real and useful:

- Balconies enumerate by type, with a count, a size, a unit price and an extension. Types are
  distinguished by dimension and by how the deck is hung, which in turn sets the knife plate count.
- Railings enumerate by run, with a linear footage and a rate per foot, segmented by location, by
  level, or by attachment method.
- Component descriptions run underneath each priced row: frame member sizes, joist sizes, decking,
  post sizes and spacing, top rail and bottom rail profiles, infill type, finish system and colour.

Because it is itemized, the counts are the primary comparable and not the totals. Two manufacturers
on one project both counted fifty one balconies while splitting them into two types and three types
respectively, and their total railing footage landed within about one percent of each other despite
completely different segmentation. The counts agree; the money does not have to.

What a proposal will state:

- Balcony count by type. Nearly always.
- Railing footage by run. Nearly always.
- The attachment method, in words: face mounted and thru bolted to studs, stem mount, fascia mount,
  top mount, mount to pavers, side mounted to a concrete parapet.
- The finish system and the colour range, and that hardware is not painted.
- Whether the railing ships as a kit to be trimmed and assembled on site.
- Lead times, as a chain of stages rather than a single number.
- That the price is for the manufacturer's own standard product. See below.

What a proposal will not state:

- Installation. Almost never in the base.
- Any reconciliation between the standard product offered and what the drawings show.
- Whether the count covers the whole building. One fabricator enumerated three named floors and
  stopped, which was visible only because it enumerated by floor.

The standard-product declaration. Three separate manufacturers in this corpus print, in near
identical words, that pricing is per their own design and standard offerings and may vary from the
plan set and specifications, with custom or non-standard material subject to a price increase
finalized at contract award. This is a market norm, not a caveat, and it is the most important
sentence in the proposal. It is not an exclusion and it is not a deviation disclosure, because it
names no specific departure. It is a grade floor asserted against the contract documents, and it
means the difference between the catalogue product and the drawn product is undiscovered at bid time
and priced after award. Where the drawings show a custom profile, expect it.

Revision habits. Rounds in this trade span years rather than months on a slow project; one
manufacturer quoted one building across roughly three years and at least four rounds. The recurring
mechanism is re-presentation rather than reprice: a thirty-two page proposal reissued as six pages
with the same product. A total diff sees nothing and the information content has changed
dramatically. Coexisting offers are also common: two proposals sent the same day off one takeoff,
differing only in whether an amenity structure is included, which the estimator logged as two columns.

## 3. Scope grain rules

Leveling sheets in this trade have run around fifty rows on a dedicated tab and around a dozen rows
where the scope is folded into a metals tab. Target forty to fifty five lines for a full package, and
give roughly half of them to railing by location.

### What enumerates

- Balcony type, each its own line, with the size, the hanging arrangement and the knife plate count.
  These three attributes travel together and the knife plate count follows from the hanging.
- Balcony count by floor or by unit type, wherever the elevations vary. Enumerating by floor is the
  one grain that makes a narrow bid visible without an independent count.
- Railing by location, each its own line, with linear footage: unit balconies, first floor patios,
  courtyard perimeter, terraces, amenity decks, roof walkway and egress route.
- Railing by attachment method, as a second axis crossing the first: stem mount, fascia mount, top
  mount, mount to pavers, side mount to parapet, face mount to blocking. Each one lands on a different
  trade's substrate and each carries a different rate. One manufacturer organizes its whole railing
  schedule this way.
- Railing by infill: plain picket, double top rail, glass, mesh, solid panel. Glass roughly doubles
  the rate per foot against plain picket in the one schedule that priced both.
- Gates, each, separately from the run they sit in, with their hardware.
- Knife plates, brackets, tie rods and post bases as their own supply line, keyed to the structural
  detail that shows them.
- Thermal breaks at the balcony connection, its own line, keyed to the architectural detail.
- Engineering, PE stamp and submittals, its own line.
- Field measurement, its own line, with the responsible party named. See section 4.
- Installation, split from supply, and split again between balconies and railings because the two
  install rates differ substantially.
- Hoisting.
- Privacy screens, fencing, pergola and cabana frames, each separately, and each flagged for whether
  it belongs to this package or to landscaping.

### What collapses

- Frame member sizes, joist arrangement, decking type, post size and spacing, rail profiles, finish
  grade and colour. All attributes of a balcony type line. They are the bulk of the proposal's page
  count and none of them is a row.
- Individual balcony locations. The type and count is the quantity.
- Fasteners and stainless hardware. Bundled everywhere.
- Shipping. One line.

### The include-or-exclude click test for this trade

The physical work is not contested; every manufacturer supplies the same object. The test that
discriminates is a partition test: could two bidders reasonably draw the boundary of this run in
different places, and would the difference be invisible in a total. It fires hardest on the railing,
which is why railing gets more rows than balconies despite being the smaller number. It also fires on
mode, since a supply column and an install column look like two bids and are two halves of one buy.

### The structural gap list

The plugs to expect on every number:

1. Installation of the balconies, at a per-balcony rate. Excluded by every manufacturer seen.
2. Installation of the railings, at a per-foot rate, and note that the general contractor's own plug
   rates for what is nominally the same operation differed by a factor of two between a unit balcony
   rail and a roof walkway rail on one sheet.
3. Hoisting.
4. Thermal breaks at the balcony connection. Carried as an allowance in a general contractor budget
   and plugged into every bidder column at a competitor's quoted value.
5. Blocking, backing and structural support for the knife plates and post bases, and the framing that
   receives them.
6. Waterproofing and flashing at every balcony attachment and every railing post penetration.
7. Field measurement, which the manufacturers put back on the general contractor by name.
8. Gate hardware, where the manufacturer excludes panic hardware.
9. The reconciliation between the standard product and the drawn product, which has no line and no
   number until after award.

## 4. Typical exclusions and qualifications

The exclusion lists here are short, because the manufacturers state their scope positively and
everything unstated is out. The qualifications carry the weight.

### The recurring exclusions, with their counterparty

| Excluded by the balcony manufacturer | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| Installation of everything supplied (nearly every bidder seen) | An iron worker, a cladding installer, or the fabricator's own install arm bought separately |
| Demolition and site work | General contractor |
| Field measurement, explicitly assigned to the purchaser | General contractor, who also guarantees the dimensions |
| Verification of all dimensions and counts | General contractor, with a change order if they differ from the plans |
| Blocking and structural support at attachments | Carpenter or steel, into which the supplied knife plates land |
| Waterproofing at attachments and post penetrations | Waterproofer or roofer, and it is a real leak path |
| Thermal breaks at the connection | Frequently unassigned |
| Panic hardware at gates | Frequently unassigned |
| Wood slats in pergola and cabana frames | Carpentry, with the frame fabricated to receive them |
| Hoisting | General contractor |

### Qualifications that are really pricing bases

- The standard-product declaration, described in section 2. It sits on the priced page, not in the
  exclusions, and it is the trade's defining qualification.
- Field measurement gates fabrication. Nothing is fabricated until field measurements are verified
  and production shop drawings are approved. One manufacturer adds that items attaching to concrete,
  pavers or brick cannot be field measured until those finishes are in place. So the fabrication
  clock does not start at award, it starts after another trade's finishes are complete, and the lead
  time chain runs from there. That is a gate chain, and it is the single most consequential schedule
  fact in the package.
- Attachment geometry constraints. One manufacturer states a minimum height above railing height
  at which its tie rod can be set. If the elevations do not allow it, the product does not fit and
  nobody discovers that until shop drawings.
- Counts are provisional by the bidder's own terms. All dimensions and counts are to be verified
  by the purchaser and a change order follows any difference, so the manufacturer's count is a
  proposal input rather than a commitment.
- Storage and delivery terms. If the purchaser cannot accept delivery within a short stated
  window, the material is warehoused at the purchaser's risk and expense at a daily rate, and the
  manufacturer may invoice for the product once fabricated. A stated minimum notice before the
  requested delivery date applies. These convert a schedule slip into a direct cost with no change
  order.
- No back charges without prior written authorization.
- Tax treatment varies: included in the price by some manufacturers, extra by others.

## 5. Furnish and install seams, and adjacent-trade overlaps

This is a seam-dominant package and the supply and install split runs through everything.

The supply and install split. The manufacturer supplies and someone else installs, and the install
half is not a rounding error. General contractor plug rates in this corpus put balcony installation
at a per-balcony rate that is meaningful against the balcony's own supply price, and railing
installation at a per-foot rate. The metals entry records that install can run a substantial fraction
of the supplied material value. A supply column and an install column on one ranking must be added,
not compared, and where the two come from related companies the addition needs checking for
double-counted overhead.

The knife plate chain. The manufacturer supplies powder coated steel knife plates, tie rods,
clevises, brackets and post bases. An iron worker or installer sets them. They land into blocking
provided by the carpenter, into studs, or into a concrete parapet. They must be set before the
exterior finish goes on. So one balcony involves four packages in sequence and the sequence is
tighter than any of the four proposals admits. The metals entry records the same chain from its side.

The railing partition. This is the largest quantity dispute in the package. Bidders diverge by more
than a factor of two on per-floor railing lengths and by roughly seventy percent on the combined roof
perimeter and egress run, on the same building, because the line between a balcony rail, a terrace
rail, a roof walkway rail and a ballasted egress guardrail is drawn differently by each of them. The
balcony count converges; the railing partition does not. Name every run explicitly and say which
package carries it.

Waterproofing. Every balcony attachment and every railing post that penetrates a deck, a paver
system or a parapet is a penetration. The waterproofing entry records these attachments as a leak
path, and every aluminum supplier seen excludes waterproofing at them.

Substrate ownership by attachment method. Face mounted to studs is the framer's substrate. Face
mounted to wood blocking under concrete topping is the framer's plus the topping installer's. Mount to
pavers is the paver system's, and it is the largest single railing run on one project. Side mount to a
concrete parapet is the concrete package's. Each attachment method row on the manufacturer's schedule
is therefore also a coordination row against a different trade.

Balcony doors. Bid in the window package. The door threshold, the balcony deck elevation and the
waterproofing at the slab edge all meet at one line and are bought from three packages.

Ballasted roof guardrail. Its own package, carried by the metals entry, and separately bundled
inside a fall protection quote on one project. Do not claim it here; do carry the row that says which
package owns the roof egress run, because that is where this package's railing footage stops.

Fencing and screens. Priced by the balcony manufacturers and noted on one leveling sheet as
assumed to sit with landscaping. Contested, and worth an explicit row.

## 6. What the drawings will not tell you

What bidders price from:

1. An enlarged balcony types sheet and a typical detail at the prefabricated balcony. Cited by name on
   the leveling sheets and by bidders.
2. The elevations, for railing runs by level.
3. The structural set, for the knife plate and bracket detail.
4. The specification, for the named basis-of-design manufacturer.
5. Their own takeoff, always. One manufacturer published its takeoff to the general contractor as a
   separate downloadable document, which made its count auditable. That is rare and worth asking for
   from every bidder.

Not deducible from any drawing:

- Which document names the basis of design. On one project the leveling sheet carries two adjacent
  rows recording that one manufacturer is named in the construction drawings and a different
  manufacturer is named in the construction specifications, both dated the same day, and every bidder
  was asked to answer both. This is a conflict between two contract documents, it decides which
  bidder is compliant, and the bidders know about it. Route it upstream as an RFI; it is not a
  leveling problem.
- How far the standard product departs from the drawn product. Undiscovered at bid and priced at
  award, by market convention.
- The balcony count. Counts diverge between bidders and against the estimator's own denominator on
  both projects here, by an eighth on one and by much more where a bidder covered only part of the
  building. Where no balcony schedule exists, the bidder authors the quantity.
- Whether the balconies are rod-hung, seat-bracket supported, or both, and how many knife plates
  each type takes. This follows from the type and drives the steel content.
- The set the bidder actually priced. One proposal in a permit-set folder names a later peer
  review set as its takeoff basis.
- Whether installation is available from the manufacturer through a third party. Several will
  arrange it if asked and none volunteers it.
- Lead time, which is a chain rather than a number: preliminary drawings after intent, engineered
  submittals after those are approved, then fabrication, plus additional time for galvanizing. The
  chain runs to roughly twenty to twenty five weeks from award in the one proposal that published all
  the stages, and it does not begin until field measurement.
- Whether the same material source sits behind several columns on the bid list.

Set stage behavior. Proposals at design development and at permit carry the same product schedule and
the same standard-product declaration; what changes between rounds is presentation length and the
addition or removal of amenity scope. Expect the counts to be stable and the package boundary to
move.

## 7. Quantity and pricing conventions

Units the trade thinks in:

- Each, per balcony, by type. The primary unit and the primary comparable.
- Linear feet, per railing run, by location, attachment method and infill.
- Each, for gates and for privacy screens.
- Each, for knife plates and post bases, derived from the balcony type.
- Weeks, for each stage of the fabrication chain.
- Pounds, for total project shipping weight. Stated by one manufacturer and there is no column for it
  on any leveling sheet, though it is the closest thing to a material-content measure the trade
  offers.
- Per calendar day, for storage if delivery cannot be accepted.
- Cost per balcony, as the normalizing denominator on a dedicated leveling tab.
- Cost per project gross square foot, where the scope is folded into a metals tab.

Cost drivers, ranked by how much movement they cause:

1. Package boundary. Whether railings, roof egress runs, fencing and screens are inside or outside
   moves the number more than any product variable.
2. Packaging mode. A supply column and a supply-plus-install column are not comparable and the
   install half is substantial.
3. Balcony count and type mix, including the hanging arrangement that sets the knife plate content.
4. Railing infill. Glass against plain picket is roughly a doubling per foot in the one schedule
   pricing both, and a value engineering substitution to standard picket rail at a roof egress walk
   was worth roughly forty percent of a selected total on one project.
5. Railing footage, which is a partition question rather than a measurement question.
6. Finish system and colour, and whether the colour is one of the manufacturer's standards.
7. Whether the drawn product is standard or custom, which is unknown at bid.
8. Gates, which price per foot far above the runs they interrupt.
9. Schedule risk carried commercially: storage rates, delivery notice windows and invoice-on-fabrication
   terms.

On spread, and what it does not prove. On the dedicated tab the ranked totals spanned about 2.7
times, and the estimator's adjustment column ranged from nothing to more than twice a bidder's own
base bid. So the adjustments moved the ranking more than the bids did, which means the spread is
mostly a statement about how much scope each column was missing rather than about price. On the
metals tab, the balcony supply columns landed within about fourteen percent of each other while the
tab's overall totals spanned more than five times, because that tab mixes structural steel,
miscellaneous metal, balconies, railings and fencing in one ranking. Both numbers are real and
neither is a market read.

What a tight balcony spread does not prove: the counts behind it may still diverge, because two
bidders can agree on a per-balcony rate and disagree on how many balconies exist. Normalize the count
first, then the railing partition, then the mode, and only then look at rates. And a converged
railing total can hide two entirely different partitions that happen to sum alike.

Two further denominator cautions. The install plug rates a general contractor applies to nominally
identical operations can differ by a factor of two across locations on one sheet, so an install rate
is location-specific rather than a trade constant. And the labor type column on these sheets carries
a packaging value such as supplier alongside the genuine labor markets, so union and open shop
comparisons on the same sheet are not like for like.

## 8. Coverage and gaps

This entry is calibrated on roughly a dozen distinct proposals from around seven bidders across two
projects, plus two estimator leveling records: one dedicated balconies and railings tab and one
metal fabrications tab where the balcony scope appears as a row block. Both projects are mid-rise
residential in one regional market, bid by one general contractor, and the proposals span design
development, permit and construction document rounds, with one manufacturer's rounds on a single
building stretching across roughly three years.

Open gaps, to be closed by later corpus rounds:

- Every manufacturer in the corpus sells an aluminum product. Nothing here is tested against steel,
  precast concrete or composite balconies, and the attachment, thermal break and corrosion questions
  are different for each.
- Only two projects, and both carry the balcony scope under different tab shapes, so the
  packaging instability in section 1 is observed twice rather than established.
- The corpus contains one substantive install-side proposal, so everything about install rates in
  sections 3 and 7 comes from general contractor plug rates and one metals entry cross-reference
  rather than from a market of install bids.
- No signed subcontract or buyout is present, so nothing shows what the standard-product
  reconciliation actually cost after award. That reconciliation is the trade's largest structural
  unknown and the corpus can only establish that it is deferred, not what it comes to.
- No shop drawing or submittal round is in the corpus, so the point at which the catalogue product
  meets the drawn detail is entirely unobserved.
- No project here has a balcony schedule on the drawings. Every count in the corpus is bidder
  authored, so the entry cannot say how this trade behaves when the documents fix the quantity.
- The corpus has not shown a project where thermal breaks were carried by a bidder rather than
  plugged, so the thermal break line is described only as a gap.
- Field measurement as the gate on fabrication rests on one manufacturer's published lead time chain.
  The other manufacturers state lead times without decomposing them, so the chain is one document's
  discipline rather than a confirmed norm.
