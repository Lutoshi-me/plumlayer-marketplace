# Fireproofing

Two regimes. On new work scope by member class and rating; on renovation scope by location and existing condition, matched to the existing material.
Sprayed fire resistive material and intumescent are two products, not variants. Give each its own row even where they protect the same members.
Split rows by member class: primary beam, secondary beam, column, brace and deck each get their own row.
Split rows wherever the rating changes, including by level, by zone and inside mechanical or vault spaces. Two ratings in one bay is two rows.
Key location to the structural sheet number, not the floor name; bidders answer sheet by sheet.
Collapse individual members, thickness, test design number and floors inside one rating zone. Thickness rides the member class row as an attribute.
Columns: write the vertical extent on the row, not just the presence. A column sprayed through three levels is not the row of one sprayed at one level.
Firestop and fire safing at penetrations and joints are not this package. Where the job buys them with the trades, tag them to the trade making the penetration.
Fire blocking and draft stopping in framed walls are not this package. Tag them to rough carpentry, and tag drywall as well where the sheet does not say who frames the wall.
A member shown encased in concrete or in a rated enclosure takes no spray. Tag the encasement to concrete and keep that member off this package.
Sprayed thermal or acoustic insulation at decks and soffits is not fireproofing even where one applicator sprays both. Tag it to insulation.
Intumescent on exposed steel is this package; the sealer or topcoat over it is painting. Tag both where the drawings expect a smooth finish.
Where the structural notes say the steel is shop primed or bare, write it as a row here and tag the primer itself to structural steel.
Where the structural sheets and the code or life safety sheet give different ratings for the same framing, that is a Question, never a picked rating.
Density or durability grade splits rows: the tougher grade where steel takes abuse in a garage, the standard grade where it is concealed.
Fire rated expansion joints are not this package; they follow the expansion joint they seal.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on four projects in one market, three of them new podium work and one a renovation; not re-mined.
