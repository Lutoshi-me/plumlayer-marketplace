# Smoke curtains

There is no smoke curtain schedule on these sets. Count protected elevator openings off the floor plans, hoistway by hoistway and level by level.
Write one row per hoistway naming its floor range and its opening count, never a single project total. Identical openings in one hoistway collapse to one row with a count.
Every opening whose protection is in question gets its own row. The lobby level and any below-grade level are the recurring ones; raise a Question rather than assuming either way.
Head box mounting is its own row: surface mounted above the ceiling, or recessed into a soffit with recessed guides. They are different products.
Elevator openings are protected by this package, not by the elevator package; elevator bidders exclude curtains. Tag the curtain to smoke curtains.
Power to the controller, conduit, boxes, disconnects and all low voltage wiring: tag electrical. The curtain arrives factory wired and stops there.
The alarm tie-in that drops the curtain is never smoke curtains and is often owned by nobody. Tag it out of this package and raise a Question on who runs it.
Blocking, backing and supports for the head box and guides: tag drywall or rough carpentry, both where the framer is unclear. Never smoke curtains.
Where the curtain is concealed, the soffit, the ceiling work and the service access panels are drywall. A recessed head box moves real work there.
Guides land in a jamb that may be stone, cast marble, tile or millwork. Who cuts that finish to receive them is unsettled; tag both and raise a Question.
Never route this scope on a specification section number; it has been cited under several, including the coiling doors' own. Route on the words elevator smoke curtain or smoke containment curtain.
Guide finish is its own row where the lobby jamb is stainless, marble or millwork.
Egress means is its own row: which side the open switch is on, whether there is one on both sides, a manual lift strap, and a test key switch.
Count group control panels as their own row where the system uses them; the panel count follows the curtain count.
Opening width and drop height ride per hoistway; they vary between hoistways and they drive the product.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on four mid-rise residential projects in one market, all of it elevator hoistway opening protection; not re-mined.
