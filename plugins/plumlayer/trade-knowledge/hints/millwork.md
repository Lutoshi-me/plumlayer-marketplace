# Millwork

Row on the architect's room number and name, then a row per element carrying the interior design detail it came from. Amenity rooms with no millwork get a row too.
Rooms whose cabinetry could be shop fabricated or catalogue, a work room, amenity kitchen, package room or leasing office, get tagged millwork and casework both.
The finish decides the seam with finish carpentry: marks that arrive shop finished, stained or veneered are millwork, marks that arrive primed for field paint are finish carpentry. Split baseboard by mark on that rule.
Split a countertop five ways: finished top, subtop, in-wall support brackets, cutouts, backsplash. Tag a stone or solid surface top to countertops; tag the subtop and the brackets millwork and countertops both.
Structure behind a millwork face is a row per condition: die walls at islands, planter carcasses at banquettes, furred back walls, faux beam and fireplace framing, restroom vanity framing. Tag rough carpentry and millwork both.
In-wall blocking and in-wall brackets are rough carpentry and are set before drywall closes; millwork locates them and does not install them.
Cabinet boxes collapse into the room element. Frameless or face frame, overlay, interior material, hinge, slide and edgebanding ride the row as attributes.
Unit entry trim, transom panels and typical elevator transom panels collapse to a row each with a count or a level range, never a row per door.
Running trim, door casing, window sills, closet rods and closet shelving are finish carpentry even on an amenity sheet. A door carrying a millwork finish mark is doors, frames and hardware.
Nail hole filling, caulking to adjacent surfaces and field painting of primed millwork are painting; the joint between a millwork piece and the wall gets its own row.
A cased opening or portal can be millwork or finished gypsum board; tag millwork and drywall both.
Curved work splits by face: front face, convex corner and return face to millwork, concave corner and the furred back wall behind it to rough carpentry.
Mailbox surround, topper and build-out are millwork; the boxes are specialties.
Banquettes split three ways: seat and back structure, upholstery labor, and the fabric or leather.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on four multifamily projects in one market; not re-mined.
