# Demolition

Two grains. A whole building coming down collapses to one row plus its seams; a building that stays standing splits by vertical zone and by operation.
Never room by room, and never a count of partitions, doors, ceilings or fixtures inside a zone. The operation on the zone is the row.
Layers belong in the row. One layer of finish is not all layers, on walls, ceilings and floors alike.
Floor removal splits by step: finish, adhesive and mastic, levelling compound, grinding, shot blasting. Demolition takes the finish; tag the later steps to flooring and demolition both.
Utilities are three rows: cut and cap at the street main is not demolition, make safe inside goes to the mechanical, electrical, plumbing and fire protection trades, and dropping and hauling what they cap is demolition.
Material the sheet flags as hazardous is abatement, not demolition, whoever prices it; tag the selective removal that reaches it to both.
Salvage is two rows: the scrap title the demolition contractor takes, and removing, stacking, storing and reinstalling what the owner wants back.
Protect, salvage and maintain are three verbs on a keynote block: one row per verb per object, never one salvage row for the block.
Foundations split three ways: slab on grade, below grade walls and footings away from property lines, and walls and footings at sidewalks, roads or abutting structures. Piles and caissons are their own row.
Demolition stops at the building footprint: pavement, curb, walks, site furnishings, light poles and fencing outside it are not demolition.
Saw cutting and coring split by purpose: new wall openings, slab openings for building systems, openings for stairs and pits, slab removal for new footings.
Shoring, bracing, needling and underpinning drawn on a demolition sheet are not demolition; tag them to support of excavation.
Existing roofing is contested: tag membrane and ballast down to deck to demolition and roofing both, and roof sealants and flashings to abatement too.
An elevator is three parties: decommissioning goes to the elevator trade, removal of cab, cables, guides, motors and switch gear to demolition.
Universal waste, meaning bulbs, ballasts, mercury switches, thermostats and chemicals, is tagged to demolition and abatement both.

coverage: derived from a book written on four projects in one market, one a large interior gut; not re-mined.
