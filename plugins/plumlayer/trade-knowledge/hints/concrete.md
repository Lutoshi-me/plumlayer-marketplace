# Concrete

Settle the regime first: new construction rows run by structural family then location, renovation rows run by structural sheet and building level.
Slabs split by thickness and by elevation, never a single slab on grade row; thickness is the priced variable.
Walls split by thickness and height band, and frost, shear, retaining, shaft and one-sided walls each take a row.
Foundations split by element type: column footings, continuous wall footings, mat foundations, uplift footings, piers, grade beams.
Supported slabs split by system and thickness: post-tensioned slab at each thickness its own row, slab on metal deck a separate row.
Formwork never takes its own row; it rides the element it forms.
Mix strength rides the element row as an attribute; never split rows by strength.
Reinforcing sits inside the concrete package: split rebar, welded wire fabric and tendons, and give coated or specialty bar its own row.
Anchor bolts, embed plates, sleeves and inserts: concrete installs them and another package furnishes them, so name the furnish side on the row.
Hold-down anchors in wood framed walls go to rough carpentry, which sets and warrants the locations; tag both where the framer is unclear.
Concrete drawn on landscape, civil, architectural and electrical sheets is still concrete: sidewalks, flatwork, topping slabs, stair fill, equipment pads.
Every site object footing takes its own row: bollards, light poles, fences, benches, bike racks, signage, trash enclosures.
Housekeeping and equipment pads enumerate by the discipline that owns the equipment, never as one pads row.
Rigid insulation at foundation walls and under slab: name the face it sits on, and tag both concrete and waterproofing where it is outside the wall.
Cementitious underlayment and gypsum floor toppings are their own package; a topping slab callout does not pull them into concrete.
Polished, stained, sealed and densified floors, shake-on hardeners and traffic coatings go to flooring.
Trench drains and the boxouts that form them go to plumbing, and the boxout is answered both ways, so tag both.
Pumping and concrete supply each take their own row; both are bought inside the package and separately.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on four projects, three new multifamily with structured parking and one renovation; not re-mined.
