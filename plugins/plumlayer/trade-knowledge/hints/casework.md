# Casework

Countertops, vanity tops, splash and subtops are their own package. Tag them to countertops even where the cabinet elevations draw them and the same company sells both.
Countertop support brackets, subtops, sink clips and sink cutouts fall between the cabinet and the top; give each its own row and tag casework and countertops both. Sinks and faucets go to plumbing.
Unit kitchens and vanities are casework. Casework drawn in amenity and common rooms is millwork, furnish and install; tag it to millwork, one row per room by the room number.
Most casework is bought supply only. Write supply and installation as separate rows and tag installation to casework and finish carpentry both.
Panels enumerate by condition, one row each: refrigerator tall end panels, kitchen surround panels, dishwasher end panels, finished sides at exposed ends, finished backs at peninsulas and at sink bases against windows, and fillers.
Cabinet boxes collapse into their kitchen or vanity; never write a row per box or per cabinet mark off an elevation.
Count unit kitchens and unit bathroom vanities as separate rows with their own counts; the bathroom count does not track the unit count.
Islands split into fixed and movable, one row each; a movable island does not reliably follow its kitchen type from unit to unit.
Blocking and backing behind wall cabinets, and the framed die walls at islands and peninsulas, are not casework; tag them to rough carpentry.
Tall linens, tall pantries, wall cabinets above toilets and medicine cabinets each get their own row; mirrors and bath accessories go to specialties.
Undercabinet and strip lighting at wall cabinets: the fixture and its moulding can ride a casework row, the circuit is electrical; tag both.
Closet, linen and laundry shelving and rod is usually not casework; tag it to finish carpentry with casework as a candidate.
Cabinet pulls and knobs get their own row counted each off the hardware marks, and pre-drilling for them gets another row.
Removable sink and cooktop bases at accessible units split from ordinary base cabinets, and split again where the drawings show removability without requiring it.
A cabinet finish keyed to a paint mark is a factory finish from casework, not painting; tag it to casework and keep the mark on the row.

coverage: derived from a book written on four multifamily projects in one market; not re-mined.
