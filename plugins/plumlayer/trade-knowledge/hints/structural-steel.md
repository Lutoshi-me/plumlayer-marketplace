# Structural steel

The package boundary follows the framing type, not the item. The same relieving angle, hanger or balcony framing tags structural steel at steel-framed levels and miscellaneous metals at wood-framed levels.
Attachment decides that boundary, never elevation. Steel not attached to the steel frame takes its own row apart from any level row, and where the framing type is unclear, tag both.
Members inside one framing condition collapse. Columns, beams, bracing and their connections at one level are one row, and levels collapse into it until the framing type or the boundary changes.
Bolts, plates, stiffeners, gussets, clips and outriggers ride the row as connection attributes, and so do grades and section sizes. A member the drawings leave unsized is one to ask about.
Metal deck tags structural steel, split by location and gauge. Deck bearing on light gauge framing or on timber is a different custody answer and takes its own row.
Stairs, handrails, guardrails, grating, ladders, loose lintels, bollards, elevator framing and angles and anything stainless tag miscellaneous metals wherever the structural sheets draw them.
Beam and beam-web penetrations for other trades take one row carrying the count, the reinforcement type and whether the work is shop or field. Their count lives in the sheet notes, not in a schedule.
Framed openings, roof opening frames and slab openings take rows apart from penetrations.
Relieving angles, shelf angles, masonry ties, seismic clips and loose lintels each take a row with the framing type on it, since the framing type is what routes them.
Elevator metals split finer than one row. Divider, hoist and safety beams tag structural steel; sill angles, guide rail supports, pit ladders and sump pit frames tag the elevator package.
Steel drawn on an architectural sheet and not on the structural sheets is bought by nobody by default. Give it its own row and ask.
Pour stop and slab edge take a row of their own by detail. Deck closure, pour stop trim and side lap treatment collapse into the deck row.
Light gauge metal framing reads in three parts: framing the wall, connecting steel to it, and running deck on it. The wall is its own package, the other two take rows here.

coverage: derived from a book written on twenty-one projects in one market, mined in a fan-out; not re-mined.
