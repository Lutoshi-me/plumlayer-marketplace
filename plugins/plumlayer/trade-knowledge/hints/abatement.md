# Abatement

Existing resilient flooring and its mastic tag together to abatement; levelling compound removal, grinding and shot blasting tag to abatement and flooring.
Demolition to open walls, drop ceilings and strip finishes so regulated material can be reached is often unowned; tag it to abatement and demo.
Lead reads two ways, licensed abatement or work under the occupational standard inside the demolition number; where the sheet does not say which, tag it to both.
Give each regulated class its own row: asbestos, lead, mercury components, PCB caulking and sealants, universal waste. Never one hazardous materials row.
Where material is marked assumed rather than tested, write the observed quantity and the assumed quantity as two rows for the same material.
Bulbs, ballasts, mercury switches and thermostats collapse to one universal waste row and their counts ride it.
Split demolition to access by what conceals the material: plaster ceilings at the deck, terracotta above ceilings, furring and perimeter walls, fixed casework.
Cutting, capping and making safe of utilities tags to plumbing, hvac, electrical and fire protection; removing and disposing of what they drop tags to abatement.
Containment setup, negative air, decontamination units, wet methods, HEPA vacuums, double bagging and labelling collapse to one line for each containment.
Do not enumerate room by room; a floor band or a named area is the smallest place a row carries.
Containment count, mobilizations, shift and the regulatory notification each earn a row of their own.
Patching and preparing abated surfaces to receive new finishes is not this package's; tag it to the trade whose finish follows.
Keep the roof a block of its own, never inside a general abatement line: sealants, flashing compounds, coping and parapet seam sealants, pitch pockets, vent pipe cement.
One roof splits three ways; tag its sealants and compounds to abatement, the existing built up roofing to demo, and the new roof to roofing.
Where a tenant area stays in occupation, write it as a row of its own so the work left out reads on the sheet.

coverage: derived from a book written on three projects in one market for one general contractor, two teardowns and one gut renovation; not re-mined.
