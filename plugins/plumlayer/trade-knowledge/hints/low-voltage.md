# Low voltage

Technology sheets carry two trades: tag the cable, jack, faceplate and device to low voltage, and the conduit, raceway, sleeve, box and poke-through to electrical.
Give each system its own rows and its own count: structured cabling, security and card access, intercom, fire alarm, area of rescue stations, responder radio amplification.
In-unit data outlets, television outlets and media enclosures are their own rows with a count per residential unit, and the enclosure row says whether a switch sits inside it.
Where the riser does not name the cable type or the count of cables to each unit media panel, ask; never write an assumed count per unit.
Outlets split by type and port count and by wall or floor mount, each one row with a count; the cable, connector, jack and faceplate collapse into the drop they serve.
Wireless access point locations are their own row, indoor split from outdoor, because they are different drops.
Card reader doors split by mount type and by single or double leaf, each its own row; a double door is not two single doors.
Cameras split by type: interior fixed dome, exterior fixed dome, fisheye, multisensor and elevator cameras are separate rows.
The cabling to the cameras is its own row apart from the cameras, and it names who carries it.
Electrified lock hardware is its own row carrying the furnish and the install split; tag it to low voltage and to doors, frames and hardware both.
Fire alarm devices, panel and programming: tag them to low voltage and to electrical both, since the power package can carry the whole family or none of it.
Read the technology series and any electronic security series together; either one can carry the readers, cameras and door groups.
Elevator cab cameras and the elevator reader are this trade; the traveling cable they run on is the elevator package.
Area of rescue assistance stations at the stairs and elevators are this trade, not the elevator package.
Equipment racks split wall mount from floor standing four post, and the head end enclosure's internal parts collapse into the enclosure package.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on three multifamily projects in one market, all bid by one general contractor; no leveling sheet exists for this trade, so the convention rows are constructed; not re-mined.
