# Roof anchors and fall protection

The roof plan does not fix the anchor count. The intended use sets it and the documents usually leave that open, so write the anchor row without a count and ask what the system is for.
Split anchors by type and name the connection on the row: end, pass-through and four-way are three products, and truss-wrap, bolt-through and deck-mount are three installations.
Take the lifeline as its own row in linear feet, and count termination and rigging sets on a second row; bidders count those independently of the cable.
Cable type is its own row: standard cable with a lanyard and a hands-free travelling system are different hardware, and the sheet often does not say which.
Anchor locations, fasteners, backer plates, thimbles, turnbuckles, shock absorbers and clamps collapse as attributes of the anchor or the lifeline. Freight is one line.
Anchor height rides the row as an attribute and it is contested: it follows the finished insulation and membrane thickness, which this package does not set.
Take engineering with the stamped shop drawings as one row, load testing as a second, the logbook and certificate as a third, and annual inspection and recertification as a fourth.
Blocking, backer plates and stiffeners at anchor locations are not this package: tag them to rough carpentry where the roof frames in wood and to structural steel where it frames in steel.
Every anchor is a roof penetration. Tag flashing, sealing and isolation coating at the anchor to roofing, and carry the anchor count over as the penetration count.
An anchor detail drawn on a metals sheet is still this package. The anchor is a listed and tested product, so do not tag it to misc metals as a fabrication.
Ballasted non-penetrating guardrail rides here on some jobs and in misc metals on others, so tag both. It is not a code-compliant permanent guardrail; keep picket railing separate.
Give davits, monorails, catwalks and tie-back-only anchors each their own row. A davit detail on the structural set and an anchor detail on the architectural set are two products.
Read the kind of a load figure before it goes on a row: five thousand pounds is the anchorage requirement and lower figures are a proof-test load.

coverage: derived from a book written on two projects, a new building and a renovation, where only one of them carried a leveling sheet; not re-mined.
