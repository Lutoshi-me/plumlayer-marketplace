# Fire protection

Fire alarm panels, detection and alarm wiring are a separate division and a separate bidder; never tag them here.
Fire protection sets flow, tamper and pressure switches and pre-action release devices; tag their wiring and the pump and controller power feed to electrical.
Every system type is its own row: wet, dry, pre-action, standpipe, special hazard. One sprinkler system line cannot be bid.
Head type splits by area, one row each: exposed garage, garage ceilings, corridors, amenity, units, balconies, stairs, ceiling to roof, concealed spaces, chutes, exterior.
Give each tagged item its own row, keyed to its drawing tag: pumps, controllers, nitrogen generation, alarm check, dry pipe, pre-action and floor control valve assemblies, backflow, fire department connections, roof manifolds.
Pipe splits by system and area, wet, dry, standpipe and residential, schedule and coating on the row. A row that says piping is not a row.
Fire extinguishers and cabinets are not fire protection; tag them to specialties.
Underground fire main from the street, trenching and the street tie-in are not fire protection; excavation and backfill go to earthwork.
Fire protection starts at the flange or a stated distance outside the wall; make the entry point its own row with that distance.
Hangers, sleeves, escutcheons, fittings and grooving collapse into the pipe rows; valve tags and signage into one identification row; drains and test connections into the drain riser row.
Coverage of concealed combustible and interstitial spaces turns on what fills the truss cavity, which insulation owns; write the row here and raise a Question.
Give every dry valve location its own row; dry systems follow unheated space, garages, trash chutes, attics, roof spaces, balconies.
The backflow assembly rides with fire protection, not plumbing.
Firestopping, coring and sleeves at sprinkler penetrations ride with fire protection, in their own rows apart from the pipe.
Ceiling tile replacement, bagging and unbagging of heads, and cutting ceilings for sprinklers go to acoustic ceilings.
Head count is its own row broken into residential, common and amenity, and garage; counts by room or floor collapse into those totals.

coverage: derived from a book drafted on four multifamily projects in one market, one of them a renovation; not re-mined.
